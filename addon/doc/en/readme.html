VisionClaw

Ask NVDA for anything in plain language, fully offline. Two small AI models on your own computer, a vision model and a coder model, break your request into small steps, agree on them, and carry them out one at a time. Nothing you do is sent to the internet. There are no accounts, keys or usage limits.

VisionClaw is based on VisionMate by Athiban Vijay and keeps everything VisionMate does. If VisionMate is installed, disable it in the Add-on Store: VisionClaw uses the same keys.

This is a first pre-release. Please double-check anything important and report what does not work at github.com/joshknnd1982/visionclaw.

Getting started

After you install VisionClaw and restart NVDA, a window opens where you choose a vision model and a coder model. The suggested ones are the safe choice; every model is offered whatever your computer is. Press Enter to download them. While they download, press Space to hear the progress. An interrupted download continues from where it stopped. Press NVDA+Alt+M at any time to change your choice.

Asking VisionClaw

Press NVDA+Alt+C, type what you want, and press Enter. The window closes so the program you were in has the focus again, and VisionClaw tells you each step as it works. Examples:

  • Make NVDA speak a little faster.
  • Turn off the typing echo and set punctuation to all.
  • How do I read the title of this window?
  • What is on my screen?
  • Press the Play button in this window.
  • Give this program a hotkey that clicks Send.
  • Write an add-on that beeps when a link gets the focus.
  • Open bbc.co.uk. Search the web for the weather in Boston. What does this page say about refunds?
  • Type garden gloves into the search box and search.
  • Which speech synthesizer am I using? What braille display do I have?
  • Switch my speech synthesizer to eSpeak.
  • Save the add-on hello key as a file.

You do not need to know NVDA. VisionClaw finds the right setting or command from your description and tells you the keys for next time.

  • NVDA+Alt+P: hear how far the request is.
  • NVDA+Alt+X: stop the request at once.
  • NVDA+Alt+C again: read everything the last request did, in the Last task box.

The two models must agree on how to handle your request before anything happens. If they do not agree, you are asked to choose. If a step does not work, you are asked whether to skip it, try again or stop. By default VisionClaw also asks before it changes a setting, clicks, presses keys or types; you can turn that off in settings.

Your settings stay yours

VisionClaw changes an NVDA setting only when you ask it to, and only the setting you asked about. A change to a setting your request says nothing about is refused. Every change is written down. Ask "what did you change?" to hear the changes, and "undo that" or "put the speech rate back" to put a setting back to what it was. Installing or updating VisionClaw changes no NVDA setting.

Programs and games NVDA cannot read

  • NVDA+Alt+O opens the overlay: the window as a list of its texts and icons. Enter clicks an item.
  • Ask "press the Start button" and VisionClaw finds and clicks it from the window's picture.
  • Ask "give this program a hotkey that clicks Send" to save a hotkey for that program.
  • Ask "call this button Play" to name a button that has no name.
  • Ask for an add-on when you need new behaviour, such as speaking when something changes.

The web

VisionClaw uses your own browser, through NVDA's browse mode. Ask it to open an address, to search the web, to answer a question about the page you are on, to follow a link or press a button by name, or to type into a field, to say how the page is laid out with its headings, to take you to a heading, link or field so you can read on from there, or to go back, forward or reload. Following a link needs no question; pressing a button asks first. Choose the search engine in settings. The models still run only on your computer; the browser goes online as it does when you use it yourself.

Making a website or a program accessible, for good

Ask "do a deep inspection of this website", "inspect this window for accessibility problems" or "what is wrong with this button". VisionClaw reads every control NVDA knows there, watches for a moment to see what changes by itself, compares the picture with the text, and tells you in plain numbered sentences what is in the way and which of it an add-on can fix. Inspecting changes nothing.

Then ask "fix all of it" or "fix number 2", or say what you want: "give this site keys to move between posts", "make NVDA+Alt+P press the Play button on this page", "make a key that reads the time", "name the focused button Share on this website", "tell me when the inbox count changes". You are told exactly what will be made and asked before it is turned on. The fixes for one website, in any browser, or for one program are kept in one add-on named fixes and then the site or program. It stays until you say "remove the add-on" and its name, and "save the add-on ... as a file" gives you a file to install for good or to share. Outside that website or program its keys do what they always do. It is built to work the same in Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox. This part is new and has not yet been tried on real websites inside NVDA.

No model writes this add-on's code: it is put together from fixed, tested parts and the list of your fixes. A control that nothing in the page's code tells apart from others cannot be named reliably, and the report says how many those are. A name taken from a button's picture is a guess, and you are told so.

Speech, braille and drivers

Ask which speech synthesizer, voice, braille display, braille tables, drivers and add-ons NVDA has and uses. Every setting of the synthesizer in use can be changed by describing it.

Switching the speech synthesizer or braille display is done with care. VisionClaw refuses a switch that would leave you with neither speech nor braille. It always asks first. After the switch you confirm that the new one works; if you do not answer within 20 seconds, it switches back by itself. It remembers drivers that gave trouble on this computer and warns you before trying them again. Commands that can silence or close NVDA are also always asked about first.

The add-ons you have installed

Ask "which add-ons do I have", "what does the Clock add-on do" or "which keys does the Clock add-on have". VisionClaw answers from what the add-on says about itself, its commands and keys where you are now, and its own help. It can also find and run an add-on's commands for you, and change its settings where the add-on keeps them in NVDA's configuration.

Working the mouse from the keyboard

Ask VisionClaw to right-click, double-click or point at an item, or to drag one: "right-click the file icon", "drag the file onto the Trash", "drag the volume slider to the right a little".

In the overlay (NVDA+Alt+O): Enter clicks, Shift+Enter double-clicks, the Applications key right-clicks, and G picks an item up; G on another item drops it there.

Pointer mode (NVDA+Alt+K) is for a control where nothing can be listed. The arrow keys move the mouse pointer in the window: 20 pixels, with Shift 4, with Control 100. Enter clicks, Shift+Enter double-clicks, the Applications key right-clicks. G holds the mouse button down and G again lets go, for dragging. W says what is at the pointer, R reads the text there, P says where the pointer is, Home goes to the middle, H repeats the keys, and Escape turns pointer mode off.

Add-ons written by VisionClaw

You never have to read, write or debug code. The coder model writes the add-on, the code is checked and compared with what you asked for, the other model reviews it, and you are always asked whether to turn it on, after being told in plain words what checking the code shows: the keys it adds, what it reacts to, which programs it works in, and whether it speaks, beeps, presses keys or clicks.

When an add-on VisionClaw wrote fails, NVDA keeps working. The add-on is paused, you hear that it is being corrected, the models debug it from the exact error, and the corrected add-on is loaded again. If that does not hold after three tries it is turned off and you are told; "fix the add-on" followed by its name lets you say what it does wrong. Generated add-ons start without restarting NVDA and come back after a restart. Ask "list what you have made" or "remove the add-on" followed by its name to manage them. Ask "save the add-on" followed by its name "as a file" to get a normal add-on file in your Documents folder, under VisionClaw add-ons, that you can install for good or share.

The check prevents accidents; it is not a sandbox. Once you say yes, the code runs inside NVDA like any other add-on. Small models make mistakes.

Describing images

  • NVDA+Alt+A: describe the current element.
  • NVDA+Alt+W: describe the current window.
  • NVDA+Alt+F: describe the whole screen.
  • NVDA+Alt+D: describe the image or image file on the clipboard.
  • NVDA+Alt+G: turn automatic descriptions of images without a label on or off.
  • NVDA+Alt+B: turn names for buttons without a name on or off.
  • NVDA+Alt+N: give your own name to the current button or overlay item.

Press a describe command once for a quick spoken description, twice for a detailed description in a window where you can ask questions about the image. Press Control to stop speaking. On a photo message in a chat app, or an image file in File Explorer, NVDA+Alt+A describes the photo itself.

Settings

NVDA menu, Preferences, Settings, VisionClaw:

  • Where the models run: Automatic, Dedicated graphics card, Vulkan, or Processor only. If the chosen place does not work, VisionClaw falls back by itself: dedicated card, then Vulkan, then the processor.
  • Two large models that do not fit on one graphics chip together: the second runs on another chip or on the processor, so both stay loaded. Turn this off to make them take turns on the best chip instead.
  • How much the models think before acting: Automatic, Off, Low, Medium, High.
  • Ask before changes, and say each step while working.
  • The search engine used when you ask to search the web.
  • Whether add-ons that fail are debugged and corrected by the models by themselves.
  • The models on this computer, with their sizes and what each is in use for.
  • See, remove or swap models: remove some or all, and choose smaller or bigger ones. NVDA+Alt+M opens the same window. You are always asked before anything is deleted, and a removed model can be downloaded again.
  • Choose or download models.
  • VisionMate's settings: automatic descriptions, names for buttons, Windows OCR, the overlay, getting ready when NVDA starts, answering in NVDA's language, and the tick while waiting.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and NVDA 2024.1 or later.
  • 4 GB of free disk space for the two suggested models, and 8 GB of RAM or more.
  • A graphics chip that supports Vulkan makes it much faster.

Privacy

VisionClaw does not collect any information. It connects to the internet only to download the models you choose and, when you press Look for newer models in the models window and say yes, to read the list of models from its own page on GitHub. Neither happens by itself. Screens, photos, requests and generated code never leave your computer.
