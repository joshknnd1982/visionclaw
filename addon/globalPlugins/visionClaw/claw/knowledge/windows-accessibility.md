# The accessibility features of Windows

## Accessibility settings of Windows: where they are and what is in them
In Windows 11, Windows+U opens Settings at Accessibility. It is grouped into Vision, with text size, Magnifier,
color filters, contrast themes and Narrator; Hearing, with audio and captions; and Interaction, with speech,
keyboard and mouse. In Windows 10 the same settings are in Settings, under Ease of Access.

## The Accessibility button in quick settings: turning a feature on or off quickly
Windows+A opens quick settings; move with the arrow keys to the Accessibility button and press Enter. It has
switches for Magnifier, Color filters, Narrator, Mono audio, Live captions and Sticky keys. Up and Down arrow
move between them and Space turns one on or off; for Color filters, Tab to its switch and press Enter.

## Narrator, the screen reader of Windows: turning it on and off
Windows+Control+Enter starts Narrator, and the same keys again stop it. Windows+Control+N opens the Narrator
settings, which have a switch called Use Narrator.

## Narrator started by mistake, or starting by itself at sign-in
If Narrator was started by mistake, press Windows+Control+Enter to stop it. If it starts by itself, open its
settings with Windows+Control+N and uncheck the choices to start Narrator after sign-in and before sign-in.

## Narrator's own key for its commands
Narrator's commands are pressed together with the Narrator key, which is Caps Lock or Insert. The Narrator key
can be changed in the Narrator settings, Windows+Control+N.

## Magnifier: switching the zoom off for a moment, and its settings
While Magnifier is on, Control+Alt+Minus switches between the chosen magnification and no magnification, and
Control+Alt with the mouse wheel zooms. Windows+Control+M opens the Magnifier settings, where it can be set to
start after sign-in.

## Magnifier views: full screen, lens and docked
Control+Alt+F gives Magnifier's full screen view, Control+Alt+L the lens view and Control+Alt+D the docked
view; Control+Alt+M goes through them in turn. Control+Alt+arrow keys move the magnified part of the screen.
Holding Control+Alt+Space shows the whole desktop for a moment. Control+Alt+I inverts the colors.

## Magnifier reading text aloud
Magnifier can read text aloud with its reading keys, which are Control+Alt unless changed in the Magnifier
settings under Reading. Control+Alt+Enter starts, pauses or resumes reading, Control+Alt+K reads the next
sentence and Control+Alt+H the sentence before. Any other key stops the reading.

## Contrast themes, once called high contrast
In Windows 11, open Settings, Accessibility, Contrast themes, choose Aquatic, Desert, Dusk or Night sky in the
list, and press Apply. Left Alt+Left Shift+Print Screen turns high contrast on or off. In Windows 10 it is
Settings, Ease of Access, High contrast.

## Making text bigger, or everything on the screen bigger
In Windows 11, Settings, Accessibility, Text size has a slider that makes only the text larger. To make
everything larger, use Settings, System, Display, Scale. In Windows 10 both are in Settings, Ease of Access,
Display: Make text bigger and Make everything bigger.

## Color filters: turning them on and off
Settings, Accessibility, Color filters turns color filters on and chooses which one. When their keyboard
shortcut is turned on there, Windows+Control+C turns color filters on and off. In Windows 10 they are in
Settings, Ease of Access, Color filters.

## Sticky Keys: pressing the keys of a combination one after the other
Sticky Keys lets a combination such as Control+S be pressed one key after the other instead of together.
Five presses of the Shift key turn it on or off. Its switch is in Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard, which
Windows+U leads to; in Windows 10, in Settings, Ease of Access, Keyboard.

## Sticky Keys or Filter Keys turning on by accident
In Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard, Sticky Keys and Filter Keys each have a setting for their keyboard
shortcut; with it off, five presses of Shift or holding the right Shift key no longer turns them on. There
are also settings to be told, or hear a sound, when Sticky, Filter or Toggle Keys are turned on from the keyboard.

## Filter Keys: ignoring brief or repeated key presses
Filter Keys makes the keyboard ignore key presses that are very brief or repeated, and can slow down the repeat
of a held key. Holding the right Shift key for eight seconds turns it on or off. Its switch and its choices,
such as ignoring quick keystrokes, are in Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard.

## Toggle Keys: a sound for Caps Lock, Num Lock and Scroll Lock
Toggle Keys plays a sound when Caps Lock, Num Lock or Scroll Lock is pressed. Holding Num Lock for five seconds
turns it on or off. Its switch is in Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard.

## Mouse Keys: moving the mouse pointer with the number pad
Mouse Keys moves the mouse pointer with the numeric keypad. Left Alt+Left Shift+Num Lock turns it on or off. In
Windows 11 its switch is in Settings, Accessibility, Mouse; in Windows 10 in Settings, Ease of Access, Mouse.
Its settings include using it only when Num Lock is on, and holding Control to go faster and Shift to go slower.

## The On-Screen Keyboard
Windows+Control+O turns on the On-Screen Keyboard, a keyboard on the screen whose keys are chosen with a mouse
or another pointing device, or with a single switch that goes through the keys. Its switch is also in Settings,
Accessibility, Keyboard.

## Live captions: text for what is said in the computer's sound
Windows+Control+L turns on live captions, in Windows 11 version 22H2 and later. They are also in quick settings
and in Settings, Accessibility, Captions. At first use, Windows asks for consent and downloads speech files;
after that captions are made on the computer and work without the internet.

## Live captions: where the window is, and captioning the microphone
In the live captions settings, Position puts the captions window above the screen, below it, or floating over
it. Under Preferences, Include microphone audio also captions your own voice; it is off again whenever live
captions starts. Filtering of profanity is under Preferences too.

## Voice typing: dictating text with Windows+H
Put the cursor in a text box, press Windows+H, wait for Listening, and speak. Say Stop listening to stop.
Windows+Alt+H moves the keyboard to the voice typing menu. Voice typing needs an internet connection and a
microphone. Punctuation is spoken by its name, such as comma or question mark.

## Voice typing commands: deleting, selecting and undoing what was said
While voice typing, say Delete that or Select that for the words just spoken, and Undo that to take back a
change. Saying Enter, Tab, Backspace or Space presses that key. In Windows 10, Windows+H opens the dictation
toolbar instead, and saying Stop dictation stops it.

## Voice access: working the whole computer by voice
Voice access, in Windows 11 version 22H2 and later, works the computer by speaking. Turn it on in Settings,
Accessibility, Speech, or search for voice access and open it. At first use it asks for a language, downloads
speech files over the internet and checks the microphone. It can be set to start before or after sign-in.

## Shortcut keys of the accessibility features of Windows
Windows+U opens the Accessibility settings. Windows+Control+Enter turns Narrator on or off and Windows+Control+N
opens its settings. Windows+Plus starts Magnifier. Windows+Control+C turns color filters on or off.
Windows+Control+L starts live captions, Windows+Control+O the On-Screen Keyboard, and Windows+H voice typing.

## Shortcut keys that turn on Sticky Keys, Filter Keys, Toggle Keys, Mouse Keys and high contrast
Five presses of the Shift key turn Sticky Keys on or off. Holding the right Shift key for eight seconds does the
same for Filter Keys, and holding Num Lock for five seconds for Toggle Keys. Left Alt+Left Shift+Num Lock turns
Mouse Keys on or off, and Left Alt+Left Shift+Print Screen high contrast.
